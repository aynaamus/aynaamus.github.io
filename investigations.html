Research initiative · 2024–present

Capital, Ownership, and Opacity

Six investigations into the global art market. Together they trace a period in which the market’s geography shifted toward Asia while ownership of artworks, and of the institutions that circulate them, became harder to see: works leaving the public record after sale, buyers standing in for financiers, companies and offshore entities between an artwork and its owner, and auction houses moving out of public-market disclosure.

The research combines public sales records, corporate and regulatory documents, legal frameworks and press reporting, documented in 132 cited notes.

Research themes

Ownership and financing

How auction houses are owned and financed, how privatisation changes what they must disclose, and how guarantees and private sales shift commercial risk.

Regulation and law

Clause-level reading of securities, art-fund and cultural-property frameworks, and the limits of international restitution law when ownership records are incomplete.

Market geography

The movement of buying power to Japan, Hong Kong and mainland China, and how private collections and museums became part of that market.
